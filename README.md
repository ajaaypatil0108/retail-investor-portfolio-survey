# How 30 Retail Investors in Ulhasnagar Build Their Portfolios

A study of portfolio management of investments: a review of portfolio-management principles and a survey of 30 retail investors in Ulhasnagar, Thane district, Maharashtra, India.

**Read it online:** [ajaaypatil0108.github.io/retail-investor-portfolio-survey](https://ajaaypatil0108.github.io/retail-investor-portfolio-survey/) · **PDF:** [Retail_Investor_Portfolio_Survey.pdf](Retail_Investor_Portfolio_Survey.pdf)

*Ajay Patil · Personal project, May 2024 · Revised October 2026*

## Key findings

| Finding | Result |
|---|---|
| Currently invest | 80% |
| Top investment choice | Mutual funds, 66.7% (then gold and silver 53.3%, real estate 50%, shares 46.7%) |
| Accept moderate or high risk | 83.3% |
| Want easy liquidity | 86.7% |
| Expect returns of 50% or 100% (period not recorded) | 33.3% |
| Review and rebalance monthly | 56.7% |
| Likely to continue investing | 86.7% |

1. **Mutual funds lead, followed by gold and silver**, ahead of direct shares.
2. **Liquidity matters, but so does property.** 86.7% want easy liquidity, yet half would include real estate, which is slow to sell.
3. **Some expect very high returns.** One in three expects 50% or 100%. The time period was not recorded; as yearly figures these would be far above what a diversified portfolio usually earns.
4. **Monitoring is frequent.** 56.7% review and rebalance monthly and 36.7% buy or sell monthly. Long term is the most common horizon (43.3%), though not a majority.

## Method

- **Primary data:** a questionnaire answered by 30 people in Ulhasnagar. The report records results for twelve questions, covering age, gender, investing status, horizon, preferred investments, income received, expected return, risk preference, review frequency, liquidity, trading frequency and intent to continue.
- **Secondary data:** books, websites, newspapers and business magazines.
- **Analysis:** descriptive. Each question is reported as the share of the 30 respondents choosing each answer.

## Limitations

The sample is small (30 people), from one city and mostly aged 18–25 (83.3%). The six respondents who do not currently invest also answered the single-choice questions, so the asset, risk and return questions reflect preferences rather than actual holdings. Only question totals were kept, so the analysis cannot compare groups.

## About this revision
